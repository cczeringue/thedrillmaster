Part historical fact. Part historical farce.

An army in need of discipline.
A Baron with other ideas.

As America turns 250, meet the Founding Fathers’ most qualified HR nightmare. It’s 1778. Washington’s army is falling apart. Enter Baron von Steuben, a Prussian outsider who knows how to drill the men.

Created by Jenny Zigrino and Caleb Zeringue, and written with Jeffrey Jay, The Drillmaster finds queer desire, belonging, and very bad behavior in the American origin story. The October 13 performance is a developmental preview of the newly revised script: catch the play while it’s still taking shape.

Is this stand-up or a play?

It’s a live staged reading of a new historical comedy. The cast performs the revised script as part of the play’s development.

How much are tickets?

The venue’s checkout lists a $35 general-admission ticket plus $6.87 in fees, for a total of $41.87. Tickets are sold by The Elysian; check its checkout for current availability and pricing.

Do I need to know American history?

No homework required. This is a mostly true comedy about the people who helped build America and the desires its history books leave out.
